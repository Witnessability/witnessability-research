      Restoration Evidence Records for AI Agent Incident Response
               draft-gilda-audit-restoration-evidence-00

Abstract

   When an AI agent system is stopped, restricted or has its credentials
   revoked during an incident, someone later decides whether it may run
   again.  Incident response guidance asks for that decision to be
   documented with the risk that remains, but no format says what the
   record must contain for a party outside the deploying organization to
   check it.  This document defines five record types for that purpose:
   an incident action authorization, a revocation sweep, a re-enable
   decision, a joint authority assignment for incidents that span
   organizations, and a revocation boundary report.  Each is an in-toto
   Statement carried in a DSSE envelope, uses the canonical form of the
   agent audit record, and is checked by ordered rules with a named
   reason for each failure.  A bundle rule set binds a re-enable
   decision to the records it cites, by digest.  A published conformance
   corpus carries a reject member for every rule that a member can
   force.

1.  Introduction

   The agent audit record [AUDIT-RECORD] records one authorization
   decision and its observed effect.  An incident adds decisions that
   record does not carry: who authorized a pause or a revocation, which
   credentials the revocation reached, who decided that the stopped
   system may resume and on what evidence, and, when two organizations
   are involved, which of them may act on which asset.  These decisions
   are made over time, by different parties, and they refer to one
   another.  The proposed charter of the AUDIT working group
   [AUDIT-CHARTER] names "authorization state over time" among the
   things its data models should represent; this document covers that
   state for the period from containment to restoration.  Section 11 of
   [AIMS] likewise requires durable audit logs covering "authorization
   decisions and subsequent remediations" and leaves their form open;
   the records here give the remediation half a form.

   Incident response guidance asks for these decisions and leaves their
   form open.  NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 [NIST-SP-800-61R3] has handlers
   work with system owners to confirm restoration (RC.RP-04) and
   declares the end of recovery "based on criteria" (RC.RP-06).  NIST SP
   800-184 [NIST-SP-800-184] describes restored assets being validated
   "before receiving approval to restore network operations"
   (Section 6.2.2) without naming the approver.  The NIST, NSA and CISA
   Key Practices for incidents in agent training and evaluation
   [KEY-PRACTICES] ask that containment layers be shown working before
   agents restart (7.3), that new controls be shown to catch a replay of
   the incident (7.4), and that the recovery decision be documented with
   the remaining risks (7.5).  None of these texts says what that
   documentation must contain for a party that trusts neither the agent
   nor its operator to check it.

   This document fixes that record.  It defines five predicate types and
   the rules a reader applies to each, and a set of rules for a bundle
   of records that together support one re-enable decision.  It
   specifies no admission policy: whether a given decision is enough to
   let a system run again is the relying party's own policy, written
   against these records.

   The goals are:

   *  to name, in each decision, the parties who held each role and the
      kind of grant each authorizer acted on, so that "the team decided"
      becomes a statement someone can be held to;

   *  to make a revocation's reach visible from the record alone, so
      that a claim wider than the evidence is refused;

   *  to make an omitted restart condition distinguishable from one the
      decision-maker chose not to address;

   *  to bind a restart decision to the authorization and revocation
      records it rests on, by digest, so the chain can be checked
      offline.

1.1.  Relationship to the agent audit record

   This document uses the canonical form, statement shape and envelope
   of [AUDIT-RECORD] and does not amend it.  The predicate type of that
   document is frozen (Section 8 of [AUDIT-RECORD]), so the record types
   defined here are separate predicate types and do not extend it.  A
   re-enable decision (R3) MAY cite an agent audit record as evidence by
   its digest (Section 5.3), which is how an enforced action and the
   decision that authorized resuming it are joined.

2.  Conventions and Definitions

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP
   14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

3.  Terminology

   Containment:  An action that limits what an agent system can do while
      an incident is handled: a pause, a restriction of a credential,
      permission or tool, or an isolation of channels.

   Restoration:  The decision to end a containment wholly or in part.

      It is separate from containment and from remediation and does not
      follow from either.

   Producer:  The party that signs a record.

   Reader:  A party that checks a record against this document.

   Relying party:  A party that decides what to do on the basis of a
      checked record, under its own admission policy.

   Bundle:  One re-enable decision record together with every record it
      names as a predecessor, recursively, and any joint authority or
      revocation boundary record the producer adds.

   Closed value:  A value drawn from a set this document or [VOCABULARY]
      enumerates.  A value outside the set makes the record malformed.

4.  Common Structure

4.1.  Statement and envelope

   A record is an in-toto Statement v1 [IN-TOTO] carried in a DSSE
   envelope [DSSE].  The statement MUST have exactly the members _type,
   subject, predicateType and predicate. _type MUST be https://in-
   toto.io/Statement/v1, and predicateType MUST be one of the five URIs
   of Section 5.

   Statements are I-JSON [RFC7493] and are canonicalized as in
   Section 4.1 of [AUDIT-RECORD], using JCS [RFC8785].  A duplicate
   member at any depth makes the statement malformed.  Times are RFC
   3339 [RFC3339] UTC times to whole seconds and are compared as
   instants.

   A member this document does not define for a type MUST be refused.
   This departs from the in-toto parsing rule that consumers ignore
   unknown fields: a revision that adds a member publishes it under a
   new predicate type URI (Section 9), so a reader of a v1 type never
   meets a member it has not been told how to read.

   Each subject member carries a digest holding only sha256.  What the
   digest is taken over is stated for each type.

4.2.  Common predicate members

   Every predicate defined here carries these members:

   incident:  An object with issuer, an https URL naming who assigns the

      identifier; id, a non-empty string; and, optionally, related, a
      list of further {issuer, id} pairs naming the same incident in
      another organization's records.  This is the term
      incident_identifier of [VOCABULARY].

   producer:  An object with id, an https URL naming the signing party.

   Where a type uses it, predecessors is a list of {predicateType,
   digest} naming earlier records of this document, where digest holds
   the sha256 of the predecessor statement's bytes exactly as carried in
   its DSSE payload.

4.3.  Closed values

   The values of response_function, pause_hold, restoration_decision,
   revocation_state, intent_binding, independence_basis and
   set_sufficiency are those of the registry whose digest is
   1c5ff80bd97237a603afc402db692ffe5abe15e4a7f9eca13f20068ada347fdc,
   published under https://w3id.org/agent-evidence/vocab/v1/registry/
   with the file name sha256-, then the digest, then .yaml.  Values that
   a section of this document lists itself are closed by this document.

   A reader MUST refuse a value outside a closed set and MUST NOT ignore
   the member.  Registration in [VOCABULARY] gives a value its identity;
   which values a member accepts is set by this document alone.

5.1.  R1: Incident action authorization

   An R1 records who authorized one incident response action, which role
   each named party held, on what kind of grant the authorizer acted,
   and whether the authority was committed before the action ran.

   The subject is the operation authorized: one or more members, each
   the SHA-256 of the operation's declared description.

   The predicate carries the common members and:

   action:  functions, a non-empty sorted list of response_function
      values (monitoring_increase, restriction, isolation, pause,
      remediation, restoration); targets, a non-empty list of {id}
      naming the agents, tools, routes or credentials acted on; and
      inducedEffects, which is one of {"declared": "listed", "items":
      [...]}, {"declared": "none_observed", "observation": {"sha256":
      ...}} or {"declared": "unknown"}. A credential revocation is
      recorded as restriction, and its reach as an R2.

   parties:  A non-empty list of {id, roles, authorityBasis?,
      independenceBasis?}. roles is a non-empty sorted list drawn from
      advised, authorized, executed and accountable.  One party MAY hold
      several roles. authorityBasis is one of role_grant,
      policy_preauthorized, contract_third_party, executive_approval and
      unknown, and MUST be present on every party holding authorized.
      independenceBasis is an independence_basis value.

   authorizedAt, notAfter:  When the authority was given, and when it
      lapses.

   executedAt:  Optional.  When the action ran.

   intentBinding:  An intent_binding value: committed_before_execution
      or asserted_after_execution.

   pause:  Optional. {hold, holdProof}, where hold is a pause_hold value
      and holdProof is {sha256} of the observation that established it.

5.2.  R2: Revocation sweep

   An R2 records which credentials of a declared population were revoked
   during an incident, which were not, and how far the producer claims
   the sweep reaches.

   The subject is the population manifest: one member, the SHA-256 of
   the list of credential identifiers the sweep was run over.
   Identifiers are opaque names; they MUST NOT be secrets, hashes of
   secrets or key fingerprints.

   The predicate carries the common members, predecessors (at least one
   R1, the authorization of the sweep), and:

   population:  credentials, a non-empty, sorted, duplicate-free list of
      identifiers.

   partition:  A list of {state, credentials} parts, state one of
      revoked, retained and unassessed.

   revocations:  One entry per revoked credential: credential;
      revokedAt; evidence, a resource descriptor {digest, uri?,
      mediaType?} of the deny record or policy change; and
      relyingParties, a list of {id, state} with state a
      revocation_state value (issued, enforced_observed,
      use_after_revocation, not_established).

   coverage:  claim, one of complete_verified, issued_after_only,
      partial and unknown; and issuedAfter, a time, present when claim
      is issued_after_only.

5.3.  R3: Re-enable decision

   An R3 records one decision about whether a stopped or restricted
   agent system may run again: the outcome, the scope restored, who
   approved it and on what independence, and, for every kind of evidence
   incident guidance asks a restart to show, either the digest of what
   was shown or an explicit statement that it was not addressed.

   The subject is the configuration restored: one or more members, each
   the SHA-256 of a baseline (model weights, harness code, container
   image, tool configuration).

   The predicate carries the common members, predecessors (at least one
   record of this document), and:

   restoration:  decision, a restoration_decision value (resume_full,
      resume_staged, remain_restricted, not_established); and
      restoredScope, present when decision is resume_staged: {agents,
      tools, routes, credentials}, each a sorted list of identifiers, at
      least one non-empty.

   approvers:  A list of {id, roles, independenceBasis}, with roles as
      in R1 and independenceBasis an independence_basis value
      (independent_sources, subject_supplied, undeclared).

   setSufficiency:  The producer's own set_sufficiency judgment of the
      evidence below: sufficient, insufficient or not_assessed.

   evidence:  An object with exactly the sixteen slots of the table
      below.  Each slot is either a resource descriptor, {"digest":
      {"sha256": ...}} with optional uri and mediaType as in the in-toto
      ResourceDescriptor, or {"notAddressed": "<reason>"} with a non-
      empty reason.  A slot MAY point at an attestation the producer
      already emits, including an agent audit record [AUDIT-RECORD].

5.4.  R4: Joint authority

   An R4 records, for an incident that crosses organizations, which
   party may act on which asset, under what kind of grant, and which
   indicators the parties share.  Restart conditions in incident
   guidance assume one owner of the restored asset; when an agent
   operated by one organization affects infrastructure run by another,
   an R4 names the owners and the assignment, so each party's R1 and R3
   records can be read against it.

   The subject is the shared indicator set: one member, the SHA-256 of
   the canonical list of shared indicators.

   The predicate carries the common members and:

   sharedIndicators:  A non-empty sorted list of {sha256} digests of
      indicators both parties hold.

   parties:  At least two entries {id, organization, correlationId,
      authorityBasis, assets}, authorityBasis as in R1 and assets a
      sorted list of asset identifiers the party may act on.

   actions:  Optional.  A list of {party, asset, record} naming actions
      already taken, where record is {sha256} of the R1 statement that
      authorized the action.

5.5.  RB: Revocation boundary

   Revoking a credential stops new work from starting under it.  It does
   not by itself stop work already in flight from committing its effect
   afterwards.  An RB reports, for one declared sink and one test
   window, whether any effect from an in-flight task committed at that
   sink's declared commit boundary after the revocation took effect.  It
   states an outcome and requires no mechanism.

   The subject is the test plan: one member, the SHA-256 of the declared
   sink and window.

   The predicate carries the common members, optional predecessors, and:

   sink:  {id, kind, commitBoundary}. kind is one of database,
      filesystem, http_endpoint, subprocess, queue and other.
      commitBoundary is a non-empty string saying at which point an
      effect counts as committed.

   observer:  {id, vantage}, vantage one of sink_side and agent_side.

   revocation:  credential, an opaque identifier; effectiveAt, when the
      revocation took effect at its authority; and timeSource, a non-
      empty string naming the clock.

   window:  {start, end}.

   counts:  Non-negative integers attempts, inFlightAtRevocation,
      postRevocationCommits, replaysAttempted and replaysCommitted.

   exclusions:  A list of {count, reason}, each reason non-empty, for
      attempts left out of the counts.

   result:  One of no_post_revocation_commit_observed,
      post_revocation_commit_observed and not_established.

7.  Security Considerations

7.1.  A signature authenticates an assertion, not its truth

   Every record here is a statement by its producer.  A conforming
   record is complete and internally consistent; it is not evidence that
   the authority named was held, that the action ran as described, that
   the cited evidence shows what its slot names, or that resuming was
   safe.  The value of the format is that a false statement is
   attributable to a named signer and that an omission is visible, not
   that falsehood is prevented.

7.2.  Absence is not read as a value

   An absent authorityBasis is not role_grant, an omitted evidence slot
   is malformed rather than satisfied, and a claim of no induced effects
   requires the observation that looked.  Each of these closes a way for
   a record to look complete because it says less.  A reader that
   supplies a default for an absent member defeats the format.

7.3.  Revocation reach

   A revocation sweep that claims more than its partition shows is
   refused (R2 rule 6).  A sweep that honestly reports issued_after_only
   or partial is well formed, and a relying party that admits a restart
   under it accepts that credentials issued earlier, or outside the
   revoked part, may still be live.  The relyingParties states
   distinguish a revocation that was issued from one a relying party was
   observed to enforce; use_after_revocation exists so that a use after
   revocation can be recorded rather than omitted.

7.4.  Work in flight

   Revocation does not stop work that is already in flight from
   committing.  An RB with a sink_side observer reports whether that
   happened at one sink in one window.  A zero count bounds a rate for
   that sink and window; it is not a guarantee for other sinks or
   windows.  An agent-side observer cannot support
   no_post_revocation_commit_observed (RB rule 7), because the agent
   process cannot see the sink's committed state.

7.5.  Authority that outlives a pause

   An R1 authorization carries notAfter.  A relying party that admits
   actions under an R1 after a pause and restart SHOULD check notAfter
   against the restart, and SHOULD require a fresh R1 for any action
   after an R3, so that a permit issued before the pause is not reused
   after it.

7.6.  Approver independence

   independenceBasis is the producer's statement.  A relying party that
   requires independence SHOULD require independent_sources and SHOULD
   resolve the named approver itself.  An approver who also stopped the
   system and approves its restart is recordable; whether that is
   acceptable is admission policy.

7.7.  Predecessor digests

   Predecessors are named by the SHA-256 of the statement bytes as
   carried in the DSSE payload.  A reader MUST hash those bytes, not a
   re-serialization, since a re-serialization that differs in member
   order or number form changes the digest.

8.  Privacy Considerations

   Records name parties by identifier and may name natural persons as
   authorizers, approvers or accountable parties.  Producers SHOULD use
   role or account identifiers where a person's name is not needed by
   the relying party, and SHOULD state in doesNotAssert what a
   distributed record deliberately omits.

   Credential identifiers in an R2 or RB are opaque names.  They MUST
   NOT carry secrets, hashes of secrets or key fingerprints, because a
   record may travel to parties not entitled to the credential.

   Evidence slots and indicator sets are bound by digest and not
   carried.  A record can therefore travel to a party entitled to check
   the decision without carrying the forensic material the decision
   rests on; that party resolves a digest only if it needs the content.
   Shared indicators in an R4 are digests for the same reason: the
   record shows the parties hold the same indicators without disclosing
   them to a reader that does not.

9.  IANA Considerations

   This document has no IANA actions.  Each predicate type is a URI
   under a namespace the author controls and requires no registration to
   be used, consistent with the in-toto Attestation Framework's
   treatment of predicate type identifiers [IN-TOTO].

   A record carries its predicate type inside the bytes its producer
   signed.  A later revision of this document MUST NOT move a URI once
   records carrying it exist, since moving it invalidates every
   signature over them.  A change to what a record of a type must carry,
   including a member or a closed value added, is published under a new
   URI with v1 read as v2, and a record valid under a v1 URI stays valid
   under every later revision that keeps that URI.

10.  Conformance

   A conforming producer emits records that meet every MUST of Section 4
   and of the section of each type it emits.  A conforming reader
   applies the rule order of Section 4.4 to each record and the order of
   Section 6 to each bundle, and reports the first failing reason code.

   A reader is not described as conforming to this revision unless it
   reaches the verdict and the reason its manifest declares on every
   member of the vectors-restart-records family of release 0.31.2 of
   [VECTORS].  That family carries 57 members, 15 to accept and 42 to
   reject, with corpus digest
   5b7f58c91b517bd7c96dcccdef0d827a4f730fd4a650b4992b362bb9925cae29.
   Each reject member changes exactly one value of an accepting control,
   and the rule it names is the first that fails.  Three rules have no
   member, and the manifest lists each with its reason: rule 3 of R4 and
   of RB, which carry no registry-defined value, and bundle rule B4,
   which would need a SHA-256 fixed point to force.  Passing the corpus
   is necessary and not sufficient; those three rules are checked by
   reading them.

   A document that references this one normatively SHOULD name the
   revision it relies on and MAY name a release of [VECTORS] as its
   conformance test.  The five predicate type URIs, the reason codes,
   and the section anchors common, rule-order, r1, r2, r3, r4, rb and
   bundles are stable across revisions and may be referenced by name.

12.  Mapping to the AUDIT Problem Statement

   The AUDIT BoF request [AUDIT-BOFREQ] and the proposed charter
   [AUDIT-CHARTER] name problems a data model should address.  This
   document addresses three of them for the period from containment to
   restoration:

   *  Authorization state over time: R1 records each authorization with
      its window, R2 the revocation of credentials and its reach, R3 the
      decision that ends a containment, and the bundle rules bind them
      in order by digest and time.

   *  References to the identities of participating entities: R1 and R3
      name each party with its role and its authority basis, and R4
      names the organizations and the assets each may act on.

   *  Verification by a party that trusts neither the agent nor its
      operator: every record is signed, every closed value is fail-
      closed, and every rule has a reason code a second reader
      reproduces.

   Action provenance for each enforced request is the subject of
   [AUDIT-RECORD]; an R3 cites such records by digest.  Policy models,
   admission policy and retention periods are left to other work.
