# Verification details and scope

Companion to the verification-coverage table in the reader manuscript. This note preserves scientific replay provenance that is summarized in the main text. Retained native records are not new runs performed for this editorial revision. Finite consistency checks and a local compiled lemma do not constitute independent scientific review or a complete machine-checked theorem.

## Finite and native checks

An additional 300 seeded generated models use one to four domains, alphabets of size two or three, nonempty joint honest supports, identity or cyclic coordinatewise transformation groups, and downward-closed failure families. The seed is 20261013. Every verdict agrees, and each generated obstruction is checked against the directly enumerated shared observation. These checks exercise the general theorem outside the deterministic identity case; they are not exhaustive over all such models.

The study checks all 63 nonempty subsets of the six candidate domains in four binding and budget configurations, for 252 portfolio checks. The projected structural result, direct record enumeration, and the multicover constraints agree. Targeted models additionally show that correlated honest supports may distinguish a claim even when their Cartesian overapproximation does not, and that a protected decisive domain can defeat the naive use of a cardinality threshold for an asymmetric failure family.

The v0.5 preparation reported a separate Wolfram Language implementation of detection and adjudication (Appendix A). The new 29 October replay used Wolfram 15.0.1 for Linux x86 (64-bit), one stateless cloud-kernel evaluation per block. A.1 returned {13536,13536,13536}, agreeing on structural and direct checks for Proposition 1 and Theorem 1. Printed A.2 checks Proposition 1 only: its 300 comparisons agree, with 291 undetected and 9 detected models. A.3 checks both statements on a second batch of 300 models and on seven benchmark rows; those outputs match the recorded expectations. Both A.4 blocks support Table 1 as described in Section 5.4. The evaluated files retain version/timing wrappers and document their deviations from the printed source. Expected comments alone are not execution receipts.

A separately retained new A.2 extension uses the same seed 20261013 and generator and adds structural and direct Theorem 1 checks. Agreement is 300/300 for each statement: 291 models are undetected and not exactly adjudicable, 8 are detected and exactly adjudicable, and 1 is detected but not exactly adjudicable. The result field all_four_agree means agreement within both structural/direct pairs, not equality between detection and adjudication. Together with A.3, this provides new computational support for both statements across the two seeded batches. Printed A.2 still contains no adjudication calculation, so the historical claim about its coverage remains corrected. The two Wolfram batches use Mersenne Twister with seeds 20261013 and 20261014 and are not the Python suite's models.

Historical v0.5 text reported detection in 3,420 exhaustive configurations, including 792 without exact adjudication, and detection in 9 and 146 models of the two seeded batches, with 5 detection-without-adjudication cases. The new A.2 diagnostics reproduce 9, but the retained A.1 and A.3 outputs do not separately report 3,420/792 or 146/5. Those remaining distributions retain historical-report status; agreement totals alone do not verify them. Historical native outputs, kernel version, and serialized seeded model corpus remain unlocated. The new extension records a Wolfram Hash value for its generated models, not a byte comparison with an independently recovered historical corpus. The benchmark thresholds in Section 7.3 also follow from the stated model and margin arguments; that derivation does not establish that a historical Wolfram run occurred.

The retained finite checker and its stored result identify their internal manuscript version as 0.3. On 29 October the unchanged checker was rerun with Python 3.12.14 and assertions enabled. Every result field matched the archived JSON except elapsed_seconds. The historical seeded result stores the seed and aggregates, not the serialized 300-model sequence, so no separate byte comparison of historical random samples is claimed.

The accompanying supplement contains source code, JSON results with obstruction examples, signed study records, and reproduction instructions. The supplement README and package manifest identify the included materials and their verification scope. These finite consistency checks share the manuscript's modelling conventions and authorship. They are not independent review, proof-assistant verification, evidence that a real system satisfies capture assumptions, or a theorem about every possible finite implementation.

## Local certificates and Lean compilation

The finite checker compares direct observation enumeration with a separate structural calculation on 13,536 exhaustive configurations, 300 seeded models, targeted correlation examples and 252 portfolios. The unchanged Python checker was rerun on 29 October as described in Section 7.4. These remain finite internal consistency checks, not independent human review or a proof-assistant check of the complete theorem. Proposition 1 is not included in that Python checker. The new Wolfram replay has the coverage stated in Section 7.4; printed A.2 does not check Theorem 1, whereas its separately retained new extension does.

Two resolution certificates separately check the local propositional implications used by the proof. A small supplied verifier checks ten resolution steps and rejects two invalid final resolvents. Eight Boolean valuations check the blend abstraction. The equality interpretation, coordinatewise inverse action, quotient characterization, quantifier lifting, and cardinality argument remain outside those certificates. On 29 October the unchanged resolution verifier was rerun on the recovered original certificates; its output was byte-identical to the archived result. Their status is deliberately narrower than a full formal proof.
